# Snowkite criteria: what makes an ideal zone

Phase 25 input. Researched 28 November 2026. This page records what sources say makes a good snowkite zone and proposes a scoring model built on it. Thresholds that appear in `config/definitions.yaml` must trace back to this page or be marked provisional.

## How to read this page

* **Evidence labels.** Each rule carries one label.
  * **Strong**: an official agency page, or several independent sources that agree.
  * **Moderate**: one credible source, or agreeing commercial sources.
  * **Weak**: a forum thread, one anecdote, a promotional page, or a source about a neighboring sport (kitesurfing).
  * **Inference**: a conclusion drawn here from physics or from the sources, with no source stating it.
* **Provenance tags.**
  * **[opened]**: the page was fetched and read. Only Wikipedia pages were opened.
  * **[search result]**: the fact appears in a search result summary. The page was not opened. A researcher should open it before the fact is displayed anywhere.
* Searches: 29 WebSearch queries (limit 30), plus Wikipedia through curl. Search summaries sometimes joined several pages. Where a quote could not be matched to one page, all candidate pages are linked.
* Style follows `CLAUDE.md`. Official names and URLs keep their hyphens.
* Most numeric guidance in snowkite sources comes from kite shops, instructors and magazines. No standards body publishes snowkite wind limits. Treat every kite size and wind range below as a rule of thumb.

## 1. Wind

### 1.1 Usable speed range

Snowkiting works in less wind than water kiting, because the rider does not need to reach planing speed.

| Claim | Source | Label |
|---|---|---|

| Riders need less wind on snow than on water. | [Ice Surf magazine, "Beyond Kitesurfing"](https://www.iksurfmag.com/?p=74960) [search result] | Weak |
| A Minnesota instructor gave a minimum of 7 to 8 mph. | [UMD, "Learning to harness the wind"](https://d.umn.edu/news/learning-harness-wind), [CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/snow-kiting-a-cool-new-minnesota-sport) [search result, quote not matched to one page] | Weak |

Label: Weak. No snowkite school publishes a maximum.

### 1.2 Kite size by wind, rider and snow

* Kite size depends on rider weight, snow conditions and intent. Deeper snow calls for a larger kite. Very deep snow on skis takes about the same kite size as kiteboarding on water. [Mack Kiteboarding snowkite FAQ](https://mackiteboarding.com/snowkitefaq.htm) [search result]. Moderate.
* Rig smaller than you think you need, especially as a beginner. [Mack snowkite size chart](https://www.mackiteboarding.com/content/snowkitesize.html) [search result]. The numeric table did not load, so no snow chart values are available here. Moderate.
* Large, highly controllable foil kites suit snow. No single kite covers the whole wind range. [Kitty Hawk, selecting snowkite equipment](https://kittyhawk.com/adventures/kiteboarding/snow-kiting/selecting-the-proper-snow-kite-equipment) [search result]. Moderate.
* Water kiting charts give a rough scale. For riders under 150 lb: 12 to 17 m in 12 to 17 mph, 9 to 12 m in 17 to 23 mph, 7 to 9 m in 23 to 29 mph. [Real Watersports](https://www.realwatersports.com/collections/kites) [search result]. Weak for snow. A 75 kg rider: 12 m covers 7 to 12 m/s, 10 m covers 9 to 14 m/s. [Windy.app](https://windy.app/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-kiteboarding-kite-size.html) [search result]. Weak for snow. Snow riders generally need a larger kite for the same wind, according to the same search summaries. Weak.
* Polar kite skiers carry three to four kites, from small 6 m² kites for strong wind to large 18 m² kites on long lines for light wind. [ExplorersWeb, Kite-Skiing for Polar Travelers](https://explorersweb.com/kite-skiing-for-polar-travelers/) [search result]. Moderate.

Meaning for the map: a zone is good when its common wind falls inside the range a mid sized kite handles, so riders of many weights can go out. A zone that needs the smallest kite on most days or the largest kite on most days scores lower. Inference.

### 1.3 Gusts and turbulence

* Gusts can run up to 50 percent above the average. Squalls can add up to 100 percent. Forecasts give averages, so peaks need a separate estimate. [Seabreeze, gust forum](https://www.seabreeze.com.au/forums/Kitesurfing/Newbies-Tips-Tricks/What-amount-of-wind-Gust-is-ok) [search result]. Weak (forum, water kiting).
* Size the kite for the strongest expected gust. In gusty wind, go 2 to 4 m smaller than in steady wind. [Surf Store guide](https://www.surf-store.com/posts/kitesurfing-in-gusty-wind-tips), [Mack on gusty conditions](https://www.mackiteboarding.com/kitesurfing-safely-in-gusty-conditions.htm) [search result]. Weak.
* One forum reply calls up to 5 knots of variation normal. [Seabreeze](https://www.seabreeze.com.au/forums/Kitesurfing/Newbies-Tips-Tricks/What-amount-of-wind-Gust-is-ok) [search result]. Weak.
* No source gives a snowkite specific gust limit. The search returned nothing authoritative.
* Wind shear, a change in speed or direction over a short distance, shows up near fronts and obstacles. [Wikipedia, Wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) [opened]. Strong for the definition.

### 1.4 Direction, fetch and obstacles

* Kiters can travel uphill and downhill in any wind direction. [Wikipedia, Snowkiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowkiting) [opened]. Moderate.
* Wind blowing uphill toward the rider is generally the best and safest. [Seabreeze, snow kiting terrain tips](https://storm.seabreeze.com.au/News/Kitesurfing/A-few-snow-kiting-tips-for-newbies-dealing-with-terrain) [search result]. Weak.
* Frozen lakes are often the best spots because wind flows unobstructed and no branches or rocks hide under the snow. [Kitty Hawk](https://kittyhawk.com/adventures/kiteboarding/snow-kiting/selecting-the-proper-snow-kite-equipment) [search result]. Moderate.
* Wind blocked or made turbulent over a hill peak makes the kite erratic and can drop it. [Wikipedia, Snowkiting, Terrain section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowkiting) [opened]. Moderate.
* Turbulence, eddies, updrafts and downdrafts sit behind ridge lines, tree lines and at ridge tops. A New Zealand guide warns to stay well clear of a ridge top with the kite up. [Seabreeze](https://storm.seabreeze.com.au/News/Kitesurfing/A-few-snow-kiting-tips-for-newbies-dealing-with-terrain), [Duotone, new kite spot](https://www.duotonesports.com/es/kiteboarding/stories/stories/new-kite-spot-what-to-watch-out-for) [search result]. Moderate (agreeing sources).
* Mountain waves form when wind crosses a mountain, escarpment or plateau edge. [Wikipedia, Lee waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_waves) [opened]. Strong for the physics.
* Surface roughness slows near ground wind. Forests have much larger roughness lengths than tundra. [Wikipedia, Roughness length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roughness_length) [opened]. Strong for the physics. Inference for kiting: treeless ground gives stronger, steadier low level wind.
* Distances for clean air behind obstacles appeared in a search summary (a European guide, source page unmatched) as a few hundred meters and called subjective. The figure is unverified and the model does not use it. A researcher needs a sourced value.

### 1.5 Thermal and katabatic winds

* Katabatic winds are downslope flows of dense cold air. They blow out of the Antarctic and Greenland ice sheets and concentrate in coastal valleys. Greenland calls them piteraq. [Wikipedia, Katabatic wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katabatic_wind) [opened]. Strong.
* Radiative cooling at night drives the common type. Anabatic winds are the warm, daytime upslope flow in calm, sunny weather. [Wikipedia, Anabatic wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabatic_wind) [opened]. Strong.
* On the Pole to Hercules Inlet leg, an operator says katabatic wind flowing north from the pole pushes the team toward the coast. [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/kite-skiing-for-polar-travelers/), [operator listing named in the result] [search result]. Weak.
* Winter sun is low, so daytime thermal wind in the US snowkite regions is probably weak. Inference. No source ties thermal wind to a US snowkite zone. Research per zone.

### 1.6 Seasonal patterns that make a place good

* Windiest months on Hardangervidda are January, February and March. [Ice Surf magazine destination guide](https://www.iksurfmag.com/travel-guides/europe/norway/hardangervidda/) [search result]. Weak.
* Hardangervidda is a treeless plateau about 6,500 km² at an average of about 1,100 m, and the largest plateau of its kind in Europe. [Wikipedia, Hardangervidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardangervidda) [opened]. Strong.
* The Great Plains are described as the windiest part of North America apart from some coasts, and North Dakota as the windiest US state. [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/kiting-across-the-great-p) [search result]. Weak.
* Idaho's Camas Prairie is described as a top spot with consistent wind and empty landscape. [Sierra Club, Go Fly a Snowkite](https://www.sierraclub.org/sierra/2009-6-november-december/feature/go-fly-snowkite) and Idaho coverage in the same search [search result]. Weak.
* A cold front is the leading edge of cooler air that replaces warmer air, and wind shear often accompanies fronts. [Wikipedia, Cold front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front), [Wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) [opened for Wind shear]. The idea that post frontal northwest wind is the usual kite wind on Midwest lakes comes from the project's draft example in `SNOWKITE.md`. No source found says it. Inference. Test with ERA5 per zone.
* Light wind years happen. One Ragnarok edition shortened to four laps in light wind. [Seabreeze, Ragnarok](https://www.seabreeze.com.au/News/Kitesurfing/When-14th-out-of-300-equals-last-place-Red-Bull-Ragnarok) [search result]. Weak. Reliability across many winters matters more than one good day.

## 2. Snow and ice

### 2.1 Surfaces

* **Frozen lakes** give open fetch and a clean surface. [Kitty Hawk](https://kittyhawk.com/adventures/kiteboarding/snow-kiting/selecting-the-proper-snow-kite-equipment) [search result]. Moderate.
* **Packed snow and ice** need less kite power than deep powder. [Kitty Hawk](https://kittyhawk.com/adventures/kiteboarding/snow-kiting), [Mack FAQ](https://mackiteboarding.com/snowkitefaq.htm) [search result]. Moderate.
* **Wind packed snow** can be the best surface, because a few days of strong wind change the surface and the morning after fresh snow with enough wind suits powder riding. [Cabrinha](https://www.cabrinha.com/blogs/news/snowkiting-tips) [search result]. Weak to moderate. Inference: wind packing both firms the snow and signals a place that gets wind.
* **Sastrugi** form where wind erodes snow, on frozen lakes, ridges and polar plains. Large sastrugi are tiring and can risk breaking equipment. [Wikipedia, Sastrugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sastrugi) [opened]. Strong. Wind packed surfaces carry this tradeoff.
* **Deep powder** is hard. One account describes hiking back knee deep after a failed launch and a rider stuck waist deep. [Sailing Scuttlebutt, Taking the Kite to the Hills](https://www.sailingscuttlebutt.com/2017/02/24/taking-it-to-the-hills/) [search result]. Weak.
* **Glacier and ice cap** surfaces suit expeditions and need crevasse awareness. Airbus describes kite skiers steering around crevasses in Greenland. [Airbus case study](https://space-solutions.airbus.com/resources/case-studies/various/snowkiting-across-greenland/) [search result]. Weak.
* **Crusted prairie**: the 2XtM expedition crossed North Dakota by snowkite, and a National Geographic piece is titled "Kiting Across the Great Plains, Snow Optional". [GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/adventure/sailing-across-the-prairie), [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/kiting-across-the-great-p) [search result]. Weak. It shows prairie travel works with thin snow and grass skis or wheels. Fields hide rocks and fences under thin snow, so the surface is rated lower than lakes.

### 2.2 Snow depth and quality

* No source gives a minimum snow depth for snowkiting. The search returned nothing. The model leaves the depth threshold `null` until a researcher finds one.
* NRCS does not publish official 1991 to 2020 snow depth normals for SNOTEL sites, because the depth record is shorter and less consistent. SWE and precipitation normals exist. [NRCS normals](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/data-and-reports/climatic-and-hydrologic-normals), [USDA blog](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/blog/new-snowpack-and-precipitation-normals-now-available) [search result]. Moderate. Depth for the model comes from NOAA normals or from computed station records.

### 2.3 Ice thickness

Minnesota DNR guidance (all [search result], pages named in the result):

* At least 4 inches of new, clear ice for walking. [Traveling on ice](https://dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/travel.html)
* 5 to 7 inches for a snowmobile or small ATV, 7 to 8 for a side by side ATV, 9 to 12 for a car, 13 to 17 for a truck. [General ice thickness guidelines](https://dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/thickness.html)
* The summary says to double the figures for white or snow covered ice. Verify against the page before use.
* DNR does not measure ice on Minnesota lakes, and thickness varies widely across one lake.

Other agencies: a Colorado page says under 4 inches of clear ice is unsafe ([CPW](https://cpw.state.co.us/icefishingsafety)). A non DNR page gave 2 inches or less as a stay on shore rule. Older or non agency pages list 8 to 12 inches for a car, which differs from DNR. Sources disagree on vehicle thicknesses. Walking minimum agrees at about 4 inches. Label: Strong for 4 inches walking.

No source gives a thickness for a kiter. A kiter moves fast, can be dragged, and carries a kite load. Treat the snowmobile figure of 5 to 7 inches as a floor. Inference. A researcher should look for kiter guidance from clubs.

### 2.4 Pressure ridges, slush and overflow

* Ridges form as ice expands and contracts with temperature swings. They are more likely in low snow years with cold spells followed by warm days. Folded ridges hide under snow. [Minnesota DNR, ice formations](https://dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/ice-formations-conditions.html), [Lake Ice, pressure ridges](https://lakeice.squarespace.com/pressure-ridges) [search result]. Moderate.
* Pressure slush forms when snow weight pushes lake water up through cracks. Overflow slush appears near shore, inlets and outlets. [Lake Ice](https://lakeice.squarespace.com/feature-summary/) [search result]. Moderate.
* Slush ice is about half as strong as clear ice. Stay off slush. [Michigan DNR](https://www.michigan.gov/en/dnr/education/safety-info/ice). Ohio State Extension advises waiting 24 hours of steady freezing after slush ([fact sheet, archived](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20090116000217/http://ohioline.osu.edu/aex-fact/pdf/0392.pdf)) [search result]. Strong (agreeing agencies).
* Snow insulates thin ice and slows freezing. [North Dakota Game and Fish](https://gf.nd.gov/ice-safety), Michigan DNR [search result]. Strong. Snow also hides cracks and open water, so wind scoured ice is easier to read. Inference from the same sources.
* Open water and weak ice sit near river inlets, around submerged objects, over gas holes and near shore as spring arrives. [Minnesota DNR](https://dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/ice-formations-conditions.html) and the Colorado page above [search result]. Strong.

## 3. Terrain

### 3.1 Flat versus rolling

* Beginners are advised to use flat terrain, since terrain also affects the wind. [Seabreeze](https://storm.seabreeze.com.au/News/Kitesurfing/A-few-snow-kiting-tips-for-newbies-dealing-with-terrain) [search result]. Moderate.
* Snowkiting works on flat ground and even uphill if wind beats the drag. Steeper slopes can block wind and cause turbulence. [Wikipedia, Snowkiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowkiting) [opened]. Moderate.
* Rolling terrain suits experienced riders who want to ride downhill and jump. The Beartooth Plateau is described as the largest alpine plateau in the US with expansive terrain for experienced riders. [Distinctly Montana](https://www.distinctlymontana.com/snowkiting-montana) [search result]. Weak.

The model rewards a large open flat or gently rolling area, because that serves the widest range of riders and carries the least terrain risk.

### 3.2 Slope angle and avalanche terrain

* Most slab avalanches start where the steepest part of the slope is 30° to 50°. A Swiss dataset of over 1,000 avalanches put 96 percent between 30° and 50°, most between 34° and 45°. Releases below 30° are rare. [Avalanche.org, slope angle](https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/terrain/slope-characteristics/slope-angle/) [search result]. Strong.
* Avalanche Canada notes slides on slopes as flat as 25° and as steep as 60°. [Avalanche Canada glossary](https://avalanche.ca/glossary/terms/slope-angle) [search result]. Strong. Sources differ on the upper end (45° or 50°) and agree on a lower bound near 30°.
* A flat meadow carries no avalanche risk by itself. A gentle area connected to steeper slopes above can still be exposed. Gullies, rocks and trees make a slide worse. [REI, Reducing Risk](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/avalanche-reducing-risk.html), [REI basics](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/avalanche-basics.html) [search result]. Moderate. REI is a retailer.
* Terrain is the easiest factor to control. [REI basics](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/avalanche-basics.html) [search result]. Moderate.
* A 2019 Fox 13 report says authorities believed a skier using a snow kite triggered a slide. No one was buried or injured. [Fox 13](https://fox13now.com/2019/12/17/another-avalanche-triggered-in-same-area-where-snowboarder-died-sunday) [search result]. Weak (one news item).
* No avalanche center page about snowkiting turned up. Why many experts avoid avalanche terrain is therefore reasoned from general avalanche education, plus the kiter specific point that a kite can pull a rider onto slopes the rider would not walk onto. The second part is Inference. Research item: ask a regional avalanche center for kite specific guidance.

### 3.3 Obstacles

* Choose a large open area. Check for hidden poles and rocks under the snow. [Kitty Hawk](https://kittyhawk.com/adventures/kiteboarding/snow-kiting), [Utah.com](https://www.utah.com/articles/post/kite-skiing-in-utah-shredding-the-gnar-er-sky/) [search result]. Moderate.
* Trees, fences and power lines tangle kites. Stay well clear. [Utah.com](https://www.utah.com/articles/post/kite-skiing-in-utah-shredding-the-gnar-er-sky/), [Sierra Club](https://www.sierraclub.org/sierra/2009-6-november-december/feature/go-fly-snowkite) [search result]. Moderate.
* Stay within walking distance of the car in case the wind dies or gear breaks. [Seabreeze](https://storm.seabreeze.com.au/News/Kitesurfing/A-few-snow-kiting-tips-for-newbies-dealing-with-terrain) [search result]. Weak.

## 4. Access

### 4.1 Roads, plowing and parking

* Skyline Drive: the main launch at Fairview Canyon has parking and restrooms, and snowplows keep Highway 31 cleared all winter. [Visit Utah](https://www.visitutah.com/things-to-do/kite-skiing) [search result]. Moderate. This is the model case for a good access score.
* A zone with plowed road access and a parking area at the launch rates highest. Plowing varies by road and year. Inference. No dataset of winter plowing was researched. Research per zone.

### 4.2 Land rules

* The Wilderness Act bans mechanical transport, and wilderness.net lists hang gliders and bicycles as examples. Wilderness Watch argues snowkiting falls under the ban and says the Park Service prohibited it in Glacier National Park's Recommended Wilderness and in other park wilderness. It also says agencies have been slow to write specific rules. [Wilderness.net](https://wilderness.net/learn-about-wilderness/stewardship/recreation.php), [Wilderness Watch](https://wildernesswatch.org/snow-kiting-in-wilderness/) [search result]. Moderate for the ban concept. Weak for each agency's reading, since Wilderness Watch is an advocacy group. Agency rules differ, so check the specific forest or park. Model rule: wilderness polygons and recommended wilderness block a zone until a researcher confirms the local rule.
* Grand Teton's Superintendent's Compendium defines kite skiing. The search summary did not show the rule. Winter closures cover Mount Hunt, Prospectors Mountain and Static Peak from 1 December to 30 April, and the park says to follow wildlife and temporary closure signs. [Compendium](https://www.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/compendium.htm), [wildlife closures](https://home.nps.gov/grte/learn/management/wildlife-closures.htm) [search result]. Moderate. Open the compendium before claiming any legality.
* Yellowstone and Rocky Mountain rules turned up nothing. Research per zone.
* National forest land outside wilderness, state land, reservoirs and private lakes each have separate rules. No source was found for a general rule. Research per zone.

### 4.3 Services, rescue and cell coverage

* Many backcountry areas lack cell service. Satellite messengers with an SOS button reach local search and rescue, and two way devices confirm help is coming. Cold drains batteries, and cell signal is often best on ridges. [Avalanche.org, satellite messengers](https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/human/gear/personal-locating-beacon-satellite-messengers/), [Avalanche.org, cell phones](https://avalanche.org/avalanche-encyclopedia/human/gear/cell-phone/) [search result]. Moderate.
* Weather can block rescue even after an SOS goes through. A Parks Canada case saw snowmobiles and a helicopter fail in a glacier blizzard. [Garmin, stuck in a blizzard on a glacier](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/blog/savedbygarmin/stuck-in-a-blizzard-on-a-glacier/) [search result]. Weak (one case).
* Nothing turned up on county rescue times for frozen lakes. Research per zone.

## 6. Hazards and etiquette

* **Snowmobiles and ice fishing.** Many snowkite lakes also serve snowmobiles and anglers. The prevailing etiquette is to talk with others before launching. [Sierra Club](https://www.sierraclub.org/sierra/2009-6-november-december/feature/go-fly-snowkite) [search result]. Moderate.
* **Fish houses and trails.** Voyageurs National Park bars ice shelters, gear and drill holes within 50 feet of an ice road edge or the centerline of a designated snowmobile trail, and asks for reflective material on shelters. [NPS Voyageurs winter safety](https://nps.gov/voya/planyourvisit/winter-safety.htm) [search result]. Moderate. Gives a sense of where trails and shelters cluster.
* **Power lines.** Keep far from power lines and fixed obstacles. Distance is the best protection. [Kite safety guides in the same search](https://sites.google.com/ocdsb.ca/kite4life/lessons/snowkiting), [Manitoba Hydro snowmobile safety](https://www.hydro.mb.ca/safety/outdoor/snowmobile/) [search result]. Moderate. Manitoba Hydro also warns of guy wires.
* **Safety systems.** Use a harness, four line kites and a control bar with a leash that cuts power on release. Same source. Weak.
* **Aircraft.** Part 101 of the FAA rules covers kites. Section 101.7 bars operating a kite in a way that creates a hazard to other persons or property. A search summary says section 101.13 limits kites near the base of clouds (500 feet) and near airports (five miles from the boundary), and that section 101.1 applies Part 101 to kites over 5 pounds on a rope. [Cornell LII, 14 CFR 101.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/101.7), [101.13](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/101.13), [101.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/101.1) [search result]. Weak until the text is read. Whether a snowkite falls under the 5 pound rule is unverified. Model rule: flag airports within five miles and research the rule per zone.
* **Other users.** Crowds, sled dogs, skiers and walkers share open lakes. No source quantified it. Research per zone.

Meaning for the map (Inference from the table):

* **Wind reliability across days.** An expedition needs a run of usable days, so measure the longest calm spell and the share of days with usable wind, and not only the share of hours.
* **Direction of travel.** Downwind or crosswind routes with steady flow, such as katabatic flow, favor kites.
* **Shelter.** Huts, camps and safe campsites out of strong wind between kite days.
* **Navigation and escape.** Open terrain gives few landmarks in whiteouts. Plan routes with bail out points.
* **Surface.** Hard wind packed snow and ice suit sleds. Sastrugi and soft snow slow them.
* **Land rules.** Wilderness blocks much of Alaska's best terrain. Check each unit, including park and refuge rules.

## 8. Proposed scoring model

A score is a computed value. It carries `confidence: estimated` and shows its inputs (`SNOWKITE.md`). Every number below goes into `config/definitions.yaml` under `snowkite`. Numbers marked **provisional** have no source. They are starting points. The model stores `provisional: true` next to them and the phase 25 build shows that flag.

### 8.1 Structure

Five scored factors add to 100 points. Three gates apply first. A zone that fails a gate shows "not rated" with the reason and no score.

| Factor | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | 35 | Every source starts with wind. No wind, no session. It is also the most variable factor and the one the data measures least precisely. |
| Surface (snow and ice) | 20 | Surface decides ride quality and, on lakes, safety. |
| Terrain | 20 | Open flat area serves the widest range of riders and carries the least risk. |
| Access | 15 | A great zone nobody can reach gets used rarely. Access is also the best sourced and most checkable factor. |
| Season | 10 | Length matters, but wind and surface already shape it. |

Weights are provisional. They reflect the order sources give the factors, and nothing sourced gives exact ratios. Test sensitivity by shifting each weight by 5 points and checking whether the top ranked zones change.

### 8.2 Gates

1. **Land gate.** Wilderness, recommended wilderness and wildlife closure polygons block a zone until a researcher confirms the local rule (section 4.2). Source: Wilderness.net, Wilderness Watch (moderate).
2. **Avalanche gate.** Any cell with slope of 30° or more connected above or beside the usable area, within a distance to be set (provisional), blocks a zone as "avalanche exposed". Between 25° and 30° the zone carries a warning. Source: Avalanche.org (strong), Avalanche Canada (strong).
3. **Ice gate (lake zones).** The zone needs a documented ice program or local source for thickness, such as a club or agency report. Walking minimum 4 inches (Minnesota DNR, strong). A kiter floor of 5 to 7 inches is an Inference.

### 8.3 Wind (35 points)

| Input | Data | Rule | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Usable hours | ERA5 10 m wind, December to March, daylight hours | Share of hours between 7 and 25 mph (3.1 to 11.2 m/s). Provisional band: floor from 7 to 8 mph (weak), ceiling below the 35 mph danger remark (weak), narrowed to account for gusts. Ideal band 10 to 20 mph counts double (Mack, Cabrinha, Visit Utah; moderate). | 21 |
| Gust control | ERA5 10 m gust field, mean wind | Penalize where gust divided by mean exceeds 1.5 (Seabreeze rule of thumb, weak). | 7 |
| Direction persistence | ERA5 wind direction | Share of usable hours inside the dominant 90° sector. Persistent direction lets riders place a launch and avoid obstacle lee zones. Inference. | 7 |

ERA5 caveat. Studies report ERA5 underestimates wind in complex and mountain terrain, overestimates weak wind and underestimates strong wind. Biases of 0.5 to 1 m/s in mean wind are common in complex terrain and coastal zones. [Copernicus ASR](https://asr.copernicus.org/articles/22/69/2025/asr-22-69-2025.pdf), [arXiv UK bias study](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04781v1), [Wind Energy Science](https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/11/13/2026/wes-11-13-2026.pdf) [search result, quotes not matched to pages]. Moderate. No validation of ERA5 10 m gusts turned up. ERA5 cells span many kilometers, so lakes and small ridges blur. Calibrate against weather station records for the established zones from phase 24 and apply a bias note to each score. Open the three papers before any bias correction.

### 8.4 Surface (20 points)

| Input | Data | Rule | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snow cover persistence | NOAA normals, SNOTEL SWE where a station is near, ERA5 or other snow depth | Share of December to March with snow cover above a depth threshold. The threshold stays `null` until sourced (section 2.2). | 8 |
| Surface type | Zone record: lake, plateau, glacier, prairie | Lake and wind packed plateau score highest, deep powder basins and prairie lower (sections 2.1). Weights provisional. | 6 |
| Ice reliability (lakes) | Research per zone | Documented thickness season. | 6 |

Zones with no snow data score `null` for this factor, never a guess.

### 8.5 Terrain (20 points)

| Input | Data | Rule | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open usable area | Terrain Tiles slope, land cover or lake polygon | Contiguous area with slope under 10° (provisional) and no tree cover. Full points at 2 km by 2 km (provisional). No source gives an area. | 10 |
| Obstacles | OpenStreetMap power lines, fences, buildings, plus tree cover | Subtract for any within a lee distance of the launch area (distance provisional, section 1.4). | 6 |
| Avalanche margin | Terrain Tiles slope | Full points when no cell at 25° or more lies within the buffer. | 4 |

Check the Terrain Tiles resolution at the zoom level used. This was not verified in the session.

### 8.6 Access (15 points)

| Input | Data | Rule | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Road to launch | OpenStreetMap highways | Paved or maintained road within a short walk of the launch (distance provisional). | 6 |
| Winter plowing | State DOT winter maintenance, research | Plowed all winter, as on Highway 31 at Skyline Drive. | 4 |
| Parking | OpenStreetMap parking, research | Parking at or near the launch. | 3 |
| Services and rescue | Distance to town, cell coverage maps (not researched), SAR research | Distance bands provisional. | 2 |

### 8.7 Season (10 points)

Count months where both wind and surface inputs pass. Four or more months earns full points (provisional). A lake zone counts months with an ice record. A plateau counts months with snow cover.

### 8.8 Calibration and the "outstanding" cut

* Run the model on the established zones from phase 24. Those zones should rank high. Large misses show a bad threshold or a bad data input.
* Rank candidates and mark as outstanding only zones that pass all gates, carry no `null` in wind, surface or access, and rank in the top decile (provisional) of rated zones.
* Show the factor breakdown and `provisional` flags on every score. A score is never a fact.
* Expedition model (phase 26) adds: longest calm spell from ERA5, share of days with usable wind, route length with hut or camp options, land rules along the route, and resupply points. Weights wait for phase 26 research.

## 9. What data cannot measure and needs research per zone

* Ice thickness and ice reliability on each lake, and kiter specific ice guidance.
* Which launches are open, and whether the owner allows kites (private land, tribal land, reservoir rules, state parks).
* The land manager's rule on kites: wilderness, national park, wildlife closure, state park (each local rule).
* Winter plowing of the access road and the parking area, and parking fees.
* Cell coverage, search and rescue response time and the nearest medical care.
* Powerlines, fences, buried obstacles and fish house clusters that maps lack.
* Turbulence behind specific ridges, tree lines and shorelines, and the sourced distance for clean air.
* Local wind patterns in a zone: thermal effects, valley channeling, how much a cold front matters. Evidence from local kiters and clubs.
* The aircraft rule for a given site, including airports, military areas and the FAA kite rule.
* Avalanche exposure on the ground beyond slope from a digital terrain model: runouts, connected slopes, regional avalanche center advice.
* Crowding and conflicts with snowmobiles, ice anglers and other users.
* Events and school presence, which show that a zone works. Phase 24 covers these.
* Expedition inputs: hut and camp options, resupply, route navigation, sled and kite setups, permits along the route.

## 10. Weakest evidence

1. **Wind range.** Floors (5 to 12 mph) and the ceiling (35 mph) come from anecdotes, a retailer and a non snowkite instructor. No standard exists. The band in the model is provisional.
2. **Gust rules.** All from kitesurfing forums and retailers.
3. **Lee side distances.** A figure appeared in a search summary with no matched page. The model uses no number.
4. **Minimum snow depth and minimum usable area.** No source. Both stay `null` or provisional.
5. **Ice thickness for kiters.** Agencies give walking, sled and vehicle figures. Kiter loads have no source. The doubling rule for white ice came from a search summary and needs a check on the DNR page.
6. **ERA5 quality for snowkite sites.** Studies of bias exist, but none tests gusts or frozen lakes.
7. **Season lengths.** Anecdotes and promotional pages.
8. **Land rules.** The Wilderness Act position rests on an advocacy group and no agency page naming snowkiting. National park rules were not found for most parks.
9. **FAA kite rule.** Seen only in search summaries.
10. **Alaska expeditions.** No documented kite traverse of a major Alaska range turned up.
11. **Avalanche and kiting.** No avalanche center page on kiting turned up. One news item mentions a kite triggered slide.
12. **All non Wikipedia pages** were read through search summaries. Open each cited page before display.
